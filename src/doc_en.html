WebSign user guide

WebSign lets you put a handwritten signature on a PDF or an image, directly in your web browser, and save the result.

What WebSign is

WebSign is a single web page. You open a PDF or an image, place one signature box on it, sign inside the box with your mouse, a pen or your finger, then save:

  • the signature alone, as a transparent PNG image;
  • the signed document, as a PDF, PNG or JPG file.

Everything is done by a floating toolbar at the bottom of the screen. It has seven buttons:

ButtonWhat it does
BoxAdds the signature box. When a box already exists, opens a menu: Erase content, Delete box, Cancel.
PenChooses the ink colour. The small bar under the pen shows the current colour.
UndoUndoes the last action.
RedoRedoes what was undone.
Save signatureSaves the content of the box as a transparent PNG image.
Save pageSaves the signed document as PDF, PNG or JPG.
ColoursSwitches the colours of WebSign for colour blindness, or back to normal colours (see Colours for colour blindness).

Buttons that cannot be used yet are greyed out (for example, Save signature stays disabled until there is ink in the box).

Privacy

Your file never leaves your device. WebSign runs entirely in your browser: nothing is uploaded, there is no server, no account, no analytics, and the page loads nothing from the web.

The two libraries WebSign uses (pdf.js to display PDFs, pdf-lib to write them) are embedded inside the page itself. The page also carries a strict security policy (Content-Security-Policy) that forbids the browser from making network connections on its behalf. Once the page is loaded, you can even disconnect from the Internet and keep working.

Nothing is stored either: when you close or reload the page, the open document and the signature are gone, unless you saved them.

Supported files

  • PDF documents, including multi-page documents, rotated pages and password-protected PDFs (see below).
  • Images: PNG and JPEG, and any other format your browser can display (for example WebP, GIF, BMP, AVIF or SVG). Photos taken with a phone are shown upright, following their orientation information.

Other file types are refused with an error message.

Opening a file

With the button

On the start screen, click Open PDF or image and choose a file.

By drag and drop

Drop a file anywhere on the page. This works on the start screen and also when a document is already open: the dropped file then replaces the current document. If you have signing work that has not been saved, WebSign asks for confirmation first.

Once a document is open, the start screen is no longer shown. To open another file without drag and drop (on a phone, for instance), reload the page or go back to the WebSign home page and open the app again.

Password-protected PDFs

If a PDF is protected by a password, WebSign asks for it. If the password is wrong, you can try again; Cancel abandons opening the file. The password is only used in your browser to decrypt the document.

Note: a protected PDF cannot be modified directly. When you save it as PDF, WebSign creates a new PDF made of images of its pages (see Known limitations).

The signature box

Adding the box

Tap the Box button . The box appears in the middle of the page that is most visible on screen. There is only one box at a time.

The box has a dashed outline while you work. This outline is not saved in the document unless you tick Keep the box outline visible when saving.

Moving the box

Drag the Move tab above the box, or drag its border. Dragging inside the box draws instead. The box always stays within the page. You can also drag it onto another page of the document: it moves to that page when you release it.

Resizing the box

Drag one of the eight handles (the four corners and the middle of each side). The box cannot become smaller than about 36 pixels on screen, nor larger than the page.

The signature is stored as lines, not as pixels: when you resize the box, the signature follows and stays sharp. If you change the proportions of the box, the signature is stretched accordingly.

The box menu

When the box exists, the Box button opens a small menu:

Erase content
Removes the ink but keeps the box where it is.
Delete box
Removes the box and its content.
Cancel
Closes the menu without changing anything. Esc does the same.

Both Erase content and Delete box can be undone.

Signing with the mouse or a finger

Draw inside the box:

  • On a computer: hold the left mouse button down and draw. A pen tablet works the same way.
  • On a phone or tablet: draw with one finger (or a stylus). Inside the box, the page does not scroll, so you can sign comfortably. Outside the box, you scroll and zoom the page as usual.

Only one finger draws at a time: a second finger or the palm is ignored while you draw. A single tap or click makes a dot. Ink that goes past the edge of the box is cut off at the border.

The thickness of the line is proportional to the height of the box, so the signature keeps the same look whatever the zoom level.

Signing in full screen

To sign with as much room as possible, tap the Full screen button next to the Move tab, above the box. The box is enlarged to the largest size the screen allows, keeping its proportions, with the part of the document under it shown as a guide. On a phone, turn it sideways to get a larger area for a wide box. Tap the Reduce button above the enlarged area, or press Esc, to go back to the document.

In full screen, a bar below the signing area offers:

  • Erase content (bin): erases the whole signature, after asking for confirmation;
  • Undo and Redo;
  • Eraser: while it is highlighted, drawing removes ink instead of adding it (only the ink: never the document). Tap it again to go back to the pen;
  • Brush size (filled circle): choose Fine, Medium (default), Bold or Extra bold. The size applies to the next strokes, and to the eraser, which is three times wider than the pen.

Everything you do in full screen appears in the box at the same place, and can be undone from the main toolbar too.

Ink colours

Tap the Pen button and choose one of nine colours: black (the default), white, grey, dark blue, red, green, pink, yellow and orange.

The colour applies to the lines you draw next; lines already drawn keep their colour, so you can mix colours in one signature.

Undo and redo

The Undo and Redo buttons cover every change: adding, moving, resizing and deleting the box, each line drawn, and erasing the content. A new action after an undo clears what could be redone.

Keyboard shortcuts:

UndoCtrl+Z (⌘+Z on a Mac)
RedoCtrl+Shift+Z or Ctrl+Y (⌘+Shift+Z on a Mac)

The history is cleared when you open another file.

Saving the signature as PNG

The Save signature button saves only the content of the box, as a PNG image with a transparent background, named signature.png. It is at least 1200 pixels wide (three times the size of the box on the document), so you can reuse it elsewhere.

Saving the document

The Save page button opens a small window with:

  1. the format: PDF, PNG or JPG (preselected: the format of the original file, or PDF);
  2. the option Keep the box outline visible, unticked by default: when ticked, a thin grey rectangle is drawn around the signature;
  3. the buttons Cancel and Save; on Android, also Share… when the browser can share files: it sends the signed file to another app (Drive, Files, mail, messaging…).

What each format contains

PDF, from a PDF
The original document with all its pages, unchanged, plus the signature added as an image on its page. Text stays text (selectable and searchable).
PDF, from an image
A one-page PDF containing the original image at full resolution (one image pixel = 1/96 inch) and the signature.
PNG or JPG, from a PDF
An image of one page: the page that holds the box (or, without a box, the page most visible on screen). The window tells you which page will be saved. The page is rendered at three times its normal size (about 216 dpi), within the limits of what browsers can handle.
PNG or JPG, from an image
The image at its original size with the signature on it.

JPG has no transparency: transparent areas become white. JPG images are saved at quality 92%.

Save

Save creates a new file named after the original with -signed added (for example contract-signed.pdf); the original file is never modified.

  • In Chrome and Edge on a computer, the browser’s own “Save as” window lets you choose the folder and the name.
  • In other browsers, a small Save as window asks for the file name: type it and tap Save. The browser then puts the file in your downloads folder, or asks where to save it, depending on its settings (in Firefox and Safari you can turn on “Always ask where to save files”). On phones and tablets, a Share… button may also be offered: on iPhone and iPad choose Save to Files to pick the folder yourself.

The Save signature button works the same way, with the name signature.png.

Saving several times in a row is safe: WebSign always starts again from the original, unsigned content it read when you opened the file, so the signature is never stamped twice.

If you try to leave or reload the page while you have signing work that has not been saved, the browser asks you to confirm.

Colours for colour blindness

WebSign normally uses green, yellow and orange. If you have difficulty telling some colours apart, choose another palette:

  • Normal colours: the default green, yellow and orange.
  • Protanopia (red-blind) and Deuteranopia (green-blind): navy blue, yellow and blue, which stay distinct when red and green look alike.
  • Tritanopia (blue-blind): dark grey, cyan and coral red, which stay distinct when blue and yellow look alike.

Choose the palette with the Colours button in the toolbar, or with the Colours drop-down at the top of the home page and of this guide. Choose Normal colours to go back to the classic colours.

Your choice applies to every WebSign page and is remembered by your browser on this device only; it is never sent anywhere. When a palette other than the normal one is active, the ink colours also show their names. The palette only changes WebSign itself: your document and the ink colour of your signature are not modified.

Using WebSign offline or from your computer

WebSign is made of five independent files. Each page contains everything it needs, so you can copy them to your computer and open them directly from the disk (by double-clicking index.html), without any web server and without an Internet connection:

index.html            home page (language choice)
app/websign_en.html   WebSign in English
app/websign_fr.html   WebSign in French
doc/doc_en.html       this guide in English
doc/doc_fr.html       this guide in French

The app pages work on their own too: app/websign_en.html alone is enough to sign documents. Keep the folders as they are if you want the links between pages to work.

Hosting on GitHub Pages

  1. Create a GitHub repository (it can be public, or private with a plan that allows Pages).
  2. Upload index.html and the app/ and doc/ folders to the root of the repository. You can also add an empty file named .nojekyll.
  3. In the repository, open Settings → Pages, choose Deploy from a branch, select the branch (for example main) and the / (root) folder, and save.
  4. After a minute, the site is available at https://<your-name>.github.io/<repository>/.

GitHub only serves the files: signing still happens entirely in the visitor's browser, and nothing is sent back to GitHub. Any other static web host works the same way.

Known limitations

  • This is not a certified digital signature. WebSign adds an image of your handwritten signature. It does not add a cryptographic signature or a certificate, and it does not prove who signed.
  • One box at a time. To sign in several places, save, open the saved file, and add the next signature.
  • Protected or unusual PDFs. When a PDF cannot be modified directly (typically a password-protected one), the PDF export is made of images of the pages, at about 144 dpi: the result looks the same but its text can no longer be selected or searched, and the file can be larger. WebSign shows a message when this happens.
  • Display of some PDFs. To keep the page self-contained, some optional pdf.js resources are not included: PDFs that rely on non-embedded Asian fonts, or that contain JPEG 2000 or JBIG2 images, may be displayed imperfectly in WebSign. This only affects what you see on screen and the PNG/JPG exports; a PDF export keeps the original pages untouched.
  • Forms and annotations are shown as they appear in the PDF but cannot be filled in.
  • PNG and JPG exports contain one page. Use PDF to keep all the pages.
  • Very large pages or images are scaled down if needed to stay within the limits of browser canvases (about 16 megapixels).
  • Browsers. WebSign was tested with Chrome on a computer and with phone emulation. It is designed for current versions of Chrome, Edge, Firefox and Safari, on computers and phones, but those other browsers have not all been tested. In DuckDuckGo for Android, the browser chooses the name of the saved file.

FAQ

In DuckDuckGo for Android, the saved file has a different name.
DuckDuckGo for Android saves the file but chooses its own file name, ignoring the one you typed; no web page can change that. Rename the file in your Files app, or use Chrome or Firefox for Android, which keep your name.
Nothing is saved in my Android browser.
A few Android browsers cannot save files created by a web page. The Save as window then shows a warning and, for images, the image itself: a long-press on it may offer to save it. Otherwise, open WebSign in Chrome or Firefox for Android.
Is my document sent anywhere?
No. Everything happens in your browser. You can check it by disconnecting from the Internet after opening the page: WebSign keeps working.
Where is my file saved?
In Chrome and Edge, where you choose in the “Save as” window. In other browsers, after you tap Save in the Save as window: in your downloads folder, unless the browser is set to ask where to save each file (on iPhone, in the Files app, under Downloads).
My signature looks stretched.
The signature follows the shape of the box. Resize the box back to its previous proportions, or erase and sign again.
Can I sign with white ink?
Yes, white is one of the nine colours. It is useful on dark documents; on a white page it is invisible.
Can I use my saved signature again?
WebSign does not keep anything between visits. You can keep the signature.png file and insert it with another program.
How do I open another file on my phone?
Reload the page, or go back to the home page with the WebSign logo at the top left, then open the app again.
